The one-day New Frontiers in Nucleic Acids symposium at Carnegie Mellon's Mellon Institute brought together researchers from Pittsburgh and Japan working on DNA and RNA structure, recognition and function. Keynotes by Prof. Naoki Sugimoto on nucleic acid chemistry beyond the canonical B-form and Prof. Alex Deiters on covalent aptamers framed a day that moved from noncanonical structures to programmable nucleic acid materials.

Invited talks covered γPNA (gamma peptide nucleic acid) recognition codes for molecular self-assembly and computing, G-quadruplex and i-motif structures in the 5′UTR of DNA methylation genes, ribozyme circular RNAs, condensates and lattice materials built with peptide nucleic acids and generative design, and RNA-driven regulation in gene control. Student talks from Carnegie Mellon, Kyoto University and the University of Pittsburgh presented RNA imaging and sensing, coacervates for mRNA and antibody delivery, engineered extracellular vesicles and DNA-based protein patterning, and the day closed with a poster session.
