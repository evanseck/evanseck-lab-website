Duquesne's annual Graduate Research Symposium brings graduate students from every school together to share their research. The 2026 symposium ran in person on March 18 in the Power Center, with poster sessions, three livestreamed oral sessions and an awards ceremony, and online all week on Symposium by ForagerOne.

The group was well represented. Sumaiya Tasnim and Jonah Babusci both gave talks in the opening oral session on RNA kissing complexes: how Mg²⁺ shifts the SARS-CoV-2 s2m kissing complex toward the A-form ahead of strand displacement, and the pathway by which the HIV-1 DIS kissing complex becomes an extended duplex. Unnikrishnan Puthumana received the School of Science and Engineering's Excellence in Graduate Research award for his work on how Mg²⁺ preorganizes the HCV X55 hairpin loop for kissing dimerization.
